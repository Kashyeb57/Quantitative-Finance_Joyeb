# Probability

Sample spaces, distributions, conditional probability, and the stochastic processes that model price movement.

## What probability means for a quant

Probability is the native language of quantitative finance. A price isn't a number you predict — it's a random variable you characterize. Option pricing is an expectation under a carefully chosen measure; risk is a tail probability; a trading edge is a distribution shifted slightly in your favor and repeated thousands of times. Get comfortable thinking in distributions rather than point forecasts and most of the field stops looking like magic.

## Where to start

- New to probability? Begin with [Probability Foundations](/docs/Probability/foundations) (the addition rule for "or") and [Conditional Probability](/docs/Probability/conditional-probability) (the multiplication rule for "and") — both fully written with runnable examples.
- [Random Variables & Distributions](/docs/Probability/distributions) is the deepest section here — start at [PMF, PDF & CDF](/docs/Probability/distributions/foundations/pmf-pdf-cdf) and work down through the discrete, continuous, and heavy-tailed families to the Central Limit Theorem. Verify the 68–95–99.7 rule and watch a skewed population's average go normal, all with live labs.
- From there, [Stochastic Processes](/docs/Probability/stochastic-processes) leads into the [Stochastic Calculus](/docs/Mathematics/stochastic-calculus) notes, which have a live GBM simulator.
- The [Probability Cheatsheet](/books) (Blitzstein & Chen) in the library is a great one-page reference.
