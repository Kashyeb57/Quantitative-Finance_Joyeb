### 14.3 Partial Derivatives

- **$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$** = rate of change in x-direction (holding y constant)
- **$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$** = rate of change in y-direction (holding x constant)

> **Geometric Meaning:** $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ is the slope of the curve formed by intersecting the surface with the plane $y=b$.

**CALCULATION EXAMPLE:**
$$
f(x,y) = x^2y + \sin(y)
$$
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2xy \quad \text{(treat } y \text{ as constant)}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = x^2 + \cos(y) \quad \text{(treat } x \text{ as constant)}
$$

**Higher partial derivatives:**
$$
f_{xx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}, \quad f_{yy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}
$$
$$
f_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x}, \quad f_{yx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y}
$$

> **Clairaut's Theorem:** $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ (if both are continuous)

### 14.4 Tangent Planes

**Tangent plane to $z=f(x,y)$ at $(a,b,f(a,b))$:**
$$
z - f(a,b) = f_x(a,b)(x - a) + f_y(a,b)(y - b)
$$

**Linear Approximation** (using the tangent plane itself):
$$
f(x,y) \approx f(a,b) + f_x(a,b)(x - a) + f_y(a,b)(y - b)
$$

### 14.5 The Chain Rule (Multivariable)

Let $z = f(x,y)$, where $x=x(t)$ and $y=y(t)$:
$$
\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}
$$

**Tree Diagram for $w = f(x, y)$ where $x = x(s, t)$ and $y = y(s, t)$:**
- **w**
  - $\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}$ ➔ **x**
    - $\frac{\partial x}{\partial s}$ ➔ **s**
    - $\frac{\partial x}{\partial t}$ ➔ **t**
  - $\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$ ➔ **y**
    - $\frac{\partial y}{\partial s}$ ➔ **s**
    - $\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}$ ➔ **t**
*(Each path contributes a product of derivatives. Add all paths to get the total derivative).*

**TWO independent variables** ($x=x(s,t), y=y(s,t)$):
$$
\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial s}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial z}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}
$$

### 14.6 Directional Derivatives and the Gradient

**Directional derivative** in the direction of unit vector $\mathbf{u} = \langle a,b \rangle$:
$$
D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} \quad \text{(dot product of gradient with unit vector)}
$$

**Gradient:**
$$
\nabla f = \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right\rangle
$$

> $\nabla f$ **ALWAYS** points in the direction of **STEEPEST ASCENT**.

**Properties of the Gradient:**
- **Max rate of increase:** $|\nabla f|$ (occurs in direction of $\nabla f$)
- **Min rate of increase:** $-|\nabla f|$ (occurs in opposite direction)
- **Zero rate of change:** occurs in any direction perpendicular to $\nabla f$ (along a level curve)

### 14.7 Optimization in Several Variables

**CRITICAL POINT:** A point $(a,b)$ where $f_x = 0$ and $f_y = 0$.

**SECOND DERIVATIVE TEST:**
Let $D = f_{xx} f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$.

- $D > 0, f_{xx} > 0 \implies$ **Local MINIMUM** (bowl up)
- $D > 0, f_{xx} < 0 \implies$ **Local MAXIMUM** (bowl down)
- $D < 0 \implies$ **SADDLE POINT**
- $D = 0 \implies$ Inconclusive

### 14.8 Lagrange Multipliers

Optimize $f(x,y)$ **SUBJECT TO** constraint $g(x,y) = k$

**METHOD:** Solve the system of equations:
$$
\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g \quad \text{and} \quad g(x,y) = k
$$

**Geometric interpretation:**
At the solution, the level curves of $f$ just barely touch the constraint curve $g=k$. At this point of tangency, their normal vectors (gradients) are parallel, meaning $\nabla f \parallel \nabla g$. Therefore, $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$, where $\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier.
