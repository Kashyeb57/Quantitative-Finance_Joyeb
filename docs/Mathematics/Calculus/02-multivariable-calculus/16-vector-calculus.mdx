$$

$$
> Assigns a VECTOR to each point in space.

### 16.2 Line Integrals

$$
\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \text{work done by } \mathbf{F} \text{ along curve } C
$$
*(Think: Force dotted with tiny displacement, summed along the path).*

<HandPlot
  title="Line Integral"
  xLabel="x" yLabel="y" domain={[0, 4]} range={[0, 4]}
  functions={[

  ]}

  ]}

  height={200}

**Evaluating Line Integrals:**
$$
\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_C P \, dx + Q \, dy
$$

Parametrize $C$ as $\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle$:
$$
= \int_a^b \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) \, dt
$$

---

### 16.3 Fundamental Theorem for Line Integrals

If $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ (the field is conservative, meaning it's the gradient of some scalar potential function $f$), then:

$$
\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = f(\mathbf{r}(b)) - f(\mathbf{r}(a))
$$

> **KEY CONSEQUENCES:**
> For conservative fields:
> 1. The integral depends **only on ENDPOINTS**, not the path taken (Path Independence).
> 2. $\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$ (The integral around any closed loop is zero).

### 16.4 Green's Theorem

$$
\oint_C P \, dx + Q \, dy = \iint_D \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) dA
$$

> Converts a LINE integral on a closed boundary curve $C$ to a DOUBLE integral over the enclosed region $D$. (Assumes $C$ is traversed counterclockwise).

<HandPlot

**Great for computing areas:**
$$
A = \frac{1}{2} \oint_C (x \, dy - y \, dx)
$$

### 16.5 Curl and Divergence

**CURL:** $\text{curl } \mathbf{F} = \nabla \times \mathbf{F}$

> Measures the **ROTATION** or "spin" of a vector field at a point.

- $\text{curl } \mathbf{F} > 0$: counterclockwise rotation
- $\text{curl } \mathbf{F} < 0$: clockwise rotation
- $\text{curl } \mathbf{F} = 0$: irrotational (often implies the field is conservative)

```text
  Curl F > 0   Curl F < 0
    (ccw)        (cw)
    ↖ ↑ ↗        ↗ ↑ ↖
    ← · →        → · ←
    ↙ ↓ ↘        ↘ ↓ ↙
```

**DIVERGENCE:** $\text{div } \mathbf{F} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}$

> Measures the **OUTWARD FLOW** from a point.

- $\text{div } \mathbf{F} > 0$: **Source** (flow out)
- $\text{div } \mathbf{F} < 0$: **Sink** (flow in)

```text
   Div F > 0      Div F < 0
    (Source)       (Sink)
    ↖ ↑ ↗          ↘ ↓ ↙
    ← · →          → · ←
    ↙ ↓ ↘          ↗ ↑ ↖
```

### 16.7 Surface Integrals (Flux)

$$
\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dS = \text{flux through surface } S
$$

> Measures how much of the field $\mathbf{F}$ "flows through" the surface $S$ (using the outward normal vector $\mathbf{n}$).

---

### 16.8 Stokes' Theorem

$$
\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S \text{curl } \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}
$$

> Relates the **LINE** integral around a closed boundary curve $C$ to a **SURFACE** integral over any surface $S$ that has $C$ as its boundary. (Generalizes Green's Theorem to 3D surfaces).

---

### 16.9 The Divergence Theorem

$$
\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E \text{div } \mathbf{F} \, dV
$$

> **Flux through CLOSED SURFACE = integral of divergence over the Enclosed VOLUME.**

*"What flows out of the surface is equal to what is created inside."*
*(This generalizes the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to 3D and unifies all the major theorems).*
