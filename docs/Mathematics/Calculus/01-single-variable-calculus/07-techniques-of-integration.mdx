### 7.1 Integration by Parts

$$
\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du
$$
*(Derived from product rule: $d(uv) = u \, dv + v \, du$)*

**CHOOSING u and dv — use "LIATE" priority for u:**
- **L** $\to$ Logarithms
- **I** $\to$ Inverse trig
- **A** $\to$ Algebraic (polynomials)
- **T** $\to$ Trigonometric
- **E** $\to$ Exponential

**Example:** $\int x \cdot e^x \, dx$
$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= x \implies du = dx \\
dv &= e^x \, dx \implies v = e^x \\
&= x \cdot e^x - \int e^x \, dx \\
&= x \cdot e^x - e^x + C \\
&= e^x(x - 1) + C
\end{aligned}
$$

### 7.2 Trigonometric Integrals

For integrals of the form $\int \sin^m(x) \cos^n(x) \, dx$:

| Condition | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **$m$ ODD** | Save one $\sin(x)$, convert rest using $\sin^2(x)=1-\cos^2(x)$, substitute $u=\cos(x)$ |
| **$n$ ODD** | Save one $\cos(x)$, convert rest using $\cos^2(x)=1-\sin^2(x)$, substitute $u=\sin(x)$ |
| **BOTH EVEN** | Use half-angle formulas: $\sin^2(x) = \frac{1-\cos(2x)}{2}$, $\cos^2(x) = \frac{1+\cos(2x)}{2}$ |

### 7.3 Trigonometric Substitution

| Expression | Substitution | Identity Used |
|---|---|---|
| $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ | $x = a \sin(\theta)$ | $1 - \sin^2(\theta) = \cos^2(\theta)$ |
| $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ | $x = a \tan(\theta)$ | $1 + \tan^2(\theta) = \sec^2(\theta)$ |
| $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ | $x = a \sec(\theta)$ | $\sec^2(\theta) - 1 = \tan^2(\theta)$ |

### 7.4 Partial Fractions

Decompose rational functions into simpler fractions:

For rational functions $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ where $\text{deg}(P) < \text{deg}(Q)$:

**CASE 1: $Q(x)$ has distinct linear factors**
$$
\frac{1}{(x-1)(x+2)} = \frac{A}{x-1} + \frac{B}{x+2}
$$
*(Multiply both sides by denominator, solve for A and B)*

**CASE 2: $Q(x)$ has repeated linear factors**
$$
\frac{1}{(x-1)^2} = \frac{A}{x-1} + \frac{B}{(x-1)^2}
$$

**CASE 3: $Q(x)$ has irreducible quadratic factors**
$$
\frac{1}{(x^2+1)(x-1)} = \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{C}{x-1}
$$

### 7.7 Approximate Integration

**MIDPOINT RULE (rectangles at midpoints):**
$$
\int_a^b f(x) dx \approx \Delta x [\bar{f}(x_1) + \bar{f}(x_2) + \dots + \bar{f}(x_n)]
$$

**TRAPEZOIDAL RULE (trapezoids):**
$$
\int_a^b f(x) dx \approx \frac{\Delta x}{2} [f(x_0) + 2f(x_1) + \dots + 2f(x_{n-1}) + f(x_n)]
$$

**SIMPSON'S RULE (parabolas — most accurate):**
$$
\int_a^b f(x) dx \approx \frac{\Delta x}{3} [f(x_0) + 4f(x_1) + 2f(x_2) + 4f(x_3) + \dots + f(x_n)]
$$

### 7.8 Improper Integrals

**Type 1: Infinite limits**

$$
\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx = \lim_{t\to\infty} \int_a^t f(x)\,dx
$$

<HandPlot
  title="Infinite domain, finite area"
  xLabel="x" yLabel="y"
  domain={[1, 8]}
  range={[0, 1.1]}
  curves={[
    { f: (x) => 1 / (x * x), color: 'ink', label: 'f(x) = 1/x²' },
  ]}
  marks={[{ x: 1, label: 'x = 1' }]}
/>

Even though the region stretches out to infinity, its **total area can be finite**:

$$
\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx = \lim_{t\to\infty}\Big[-\tfrac{1}{x}\Big]_1^t = \lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(-\tfrac{1}{t}+1\Big) = 1 \quad \Longleftarrow \text{ converges!}
$$

But $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \infty$ — this one **diverges**, "just barely."

**Type 2: Discontinuous integrands**

For $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ where $f$ blows up at $a$:

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{t\to a^+} \int_t^b f(x)\,dx
$$
