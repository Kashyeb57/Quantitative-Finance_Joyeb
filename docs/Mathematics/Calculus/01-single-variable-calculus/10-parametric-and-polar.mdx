### 10.1–10.2 Parametric Curves

Instead of $y=f(x)$, use:
$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= f(t) \\
y &= g(t)
\end{aligned} \quad (t \text{ is the parameter, often time})
$$

**Example:** Circle $x = \cos(t)$, $y = \sin(t)$, $0 \le t \le 2\pi$

**Derivatives:**
$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}
$$

**Arc Length:**
$$
L = \int \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt
$$

### 10.3–10.4 Polar Coordinates

Point $P = (r, \theta)$ instead of $(x, y)$ where:
- $r =$ distance from origin
- $\theta =$ angle from positive x-axis

**Conversions:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= r \cos(\theta) &\quad r^2 &= x^2 + y^2 \\
y &= r \sin(\theta) &\quad \tan(\theta) &= \frac{y}{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Common curves:**
- $r = a$ → circle of radius a
- $r = a\cos(\theta)$ → circle through origin
- $r = 1+\cos(\theta)$ → cardioid (heart shape)

**Area in polar:**
$$
A = \int_\alpha^\beta \frac{1}{2} r^2 \, d\theta
$$

### 10.5–10.6 Conic Sections

All conics: locus of points where $\frac{\text{dist to focus}}{\text{dist to directrix}} = e$

| Eccentricity $e$ | Conic Section |
|---|---|
| $e = 0$ | Circle |
| $0 < e < 1$ | Ellipse |
| $e = 1$ | Parabola |
| $e > 1$ | Hyperbola |

**Polar form:** r = ed/(1 ± e·cos θ) or r = ed/(1 ± e·sin θ)
